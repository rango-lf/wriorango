bury him

july 11, 2024

By June, I thought I would’ve figured out

what it meant to be a man: learning when

to say bro, dude, & fuck & whittling skinny

spare sticks into something I can kill with.

The damp days became stagnant:

my AC shuddered to its bitter end, my room

smelled of death & mid-summer existentialism.

I rotted in bed under the heaviness of

growing pains, wondering if I really meant it.

Growing up, I knew I didn’t want to become

anything horrible: no trembling walls or shattered

whiskey glasses. There was that pre-teen summer:

the girls & I squatting on the yellow river bank, drawing up

our flashlights. We wondered if what the woman-mouth named

monster sat on the other side & what death they would

surely bring. I couldn’t keep myself from staring down at my palms.

When I outgrew my gender & jeans, my biggest fear was the

hair they said would sprout from my arms & chest,

women curving around my path like a split school

of minnows. In the night I dreamed for a different kind of

manhood, imaginary & impossible as a wet July

afternoon. I prayed for a different kind of puberty.

I wanted manhood soft & unscorned without being

named traitor. I wanted my mother to look me in

the eyes without that sorrowful, pinched look.

 

Which part is predator? Which part is prey? I imagine

the scene of our old kitchen of chipped china &

chaos, where my father would pull all the oxygen

from my mother’s lungs. She would be standing there

–the scene painted nectar by sundown–holding me in her

dishwater-pruned hands. My grandmother is there too.

She says: bury it, or it’ll grow up to become

something monstrous.
